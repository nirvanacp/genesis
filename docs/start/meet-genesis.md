# Meet Genesis

[Learning path](../README.md) · Step 1

Genesis runs websites and lets your custom app change how you use them.

The website keeps running underneath. It supplies its content, sign-in, forms, and account actions. Your app can change the layout, add controls, and provide screens of its own.

## Picture a reading app

You open a news website inside Genesis.

- Stories have larger text and more space.
- A custom Save button adds a story to your own collection.
- A Saved screen groups stories by topic.
- Opening a story takes you to its live website.

That is an app you could ask an agent to build. The collection and grouping need their own implementation; Genesis does not add them to every website automatically.

## A few words you will see

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Genesis | The Android app that runs your custom apps and their websites |
| Applet | One custom app you open from Genesis's Apps list |
| Source | A live website used by your app |
| App page | A screen your app owns, such as a combined Saved library |
| Coding agent | An AI coding tool that reads your request and changes your app's files |

One applet can contain several sources and app pages. You can also have several separate applets in Genesis.

## Who does what?

You decide how the app should work. Your coding agent writes and checks its code. Genesis runs the result.

The original website still controls its accounts and service. A custom layout does not remove a subscription requirement, grant account access, or guarantee that every website will work.

Genesis aims for an app that feels at home on your phone. That does not mean your app automatically has every feature of a website's official native app.
